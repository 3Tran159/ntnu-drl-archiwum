# NEXT STAGE: Moving-Flap Hydrodynamic Model and Validation

Continue the existing NTNU Active Flap CFD project in STAR-CCM+ 2606.

The previous proof of concept was successful:
- 44,763 cells, Overset Mesh
- Moving flap at x/c = 0.80
- ±5° motion verified
- Initial transient completed
- Working reference: `flap_poc_2606_20261102/FlapPOC_2606_working.sim`

**Objective:** Upgrade this working mechanical demonstrator into a numerically credible hydrodynamic model suitable for generating CFD training data.

Do not build a new motion architecture. Reuse the proven Overset implementation.

## 1. Improve flap geometry

- Preserve the native NACA 66-012 modified contour.
- Keep the hinge at x/c = 0.80.
- Reduce the present 17.85 mm gap.
- Investigate an initial clearance of approximately 2–3 mm, but adjust it if required for mesh reliability.
- Verify that the moving flap never intersects the fixed foil throughout ±5°.
- Make gap width and hinge geometry parametrically adjustable.
- Ensure that sufficient cells resolve the gap and that Overset donor/acceptor connectivity remains valid.

Preserve the successful POC and create a separate working copy.

## 2. Build a credible calm-water CFD model

Use:
- U = 18 m/s
- Water density = 1000 kg/m³
- Dynamic viscosity = 0.001 Pa·s
- URANS SST k-ω
- Quasi-2D configuration
- Initially single-phase flow, without waves

Reuse the lessons from our previous M10–M13 mesh campaign. Start with a moderately refined mesh, not an unnecessarily expensive one.

Check mesh quality, wall treatment, prism layers, Overset connectivity and timestep sensitivity.

Do not assume that the previous coarse-mesh force results are physically valid.

## 3. Validation stage V1: Fixed flap

Start with δ = 0°.

Compare:
1. Original unmodified NACA 66-012 geometry, C01.
2. New split geometry with the flap fixed at 0°.

Our previous accepted reference CL was approximately 0.31566, but use the actual C01 data and consistent normalization.

Determine how much of any CL/CD difference originates from the flap gap, modified geometry and Overset interpolation.

Next, run static deflections of −2°, +2°, −5° and +5°.

Export CL, CD, Fx, Fz, hinge moment and pressure distribution.

Check whether trends agree with established flapped-airfoil theory and relevant experimental studies.

## 4. Validation stage V2: Dynamic flap

After the stationary cases pass:

- Test harmonic flap motion at ±2°.
- Test at least two excitation frequencies.
- Extend to ±5° if stable.
- Record the complete time histories.
- Check amplitude and phase lag of CL, CD and hinge moment.
- Compare the response against appropriate unsteady airfoil theory within its assumptions.

Perform a limited mesh and timestep sensitivity study before approving the results for surrogate-model training.

## 5. Mandatory constraint: Cavitation avoidance

Implement pressure-based cavitation monitoring.

For every physical timestep, evaluate:

`p_absolute_min > p_vapor(T) + safety_margin`

Requirements:
- Check fixed foil, flap, gap and nearby liquid region.
- Use correct absolute pressure and hydrostatic reference.
- Export minimum pressure and its spatial location.
- Record the safety margin and when a violation occurs.
- Distinguish preliminary pressure-based screening from actual cavitation-model validation.

Do not accept an apparent drag reduction achieved at the expense of cavitation risk.

## 6. Mandatory constraint: Free-surface clearance

Our hydrofoil must not approach the free surface so closely that ventilation, loss of lift or strong unintended free-surface effects invalidate the operating condition.

Define a minimum instantaneous vertical clearance:

`h_clear(t) = z_free_surface(t) - z_highest_foil_point(t)`

using an upward-positive vertical coordinate.

For the later VOF cases:

- Monitor undisturbed wave elevation and local free-surface deformation.
- Track the moving foil's highest point.
- Calculate h_clear/c throughout the simulation.
- Detect any potential air connection or ventilation.
- Introduce a configurable minimum-clearance requirement.
- Perform a sensitivity study with immersion depth instead of assuming one universal safe h/c value.

Do not use a fixed depth of two chords as an automatic acceptance criterion. Establish the appropriate operating envelope numerically.

Cavitation avoidance and sufficient free-surface clearance are independent constraints. Both must be satisfied.

## 7. Experimental validation references

Use the following as methodological benchmarks. Where the experimental geometry differs, create a separate validation case rather than directly comparing absolute values with our NACA 66-012.

- Wilson (1982): Experimental Performance of a Flapped Hydrofoil in Calm Water at Low Froude Numbers.
- Pernod et al. (2023): Free-Surface Effects on Two-Dimensional Hydrofoils by RANS-VOF Simulations.
- Conolly (1963): Flapped Hydrofoils in Waves, Subcavitating Flow.
- Portal-Porras et al. (2023): Active Flow Control on Airfoils by Reinforcement Learning.
- Mohammed Arab et al. (2022): Effects on Cavitation Inception of Leading and Trailing Edge Flaps.

Separate numerical verification (mesh/timestep) from experimental validation.
