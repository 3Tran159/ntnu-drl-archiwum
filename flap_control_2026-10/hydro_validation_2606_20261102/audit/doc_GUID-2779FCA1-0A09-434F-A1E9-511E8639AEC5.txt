 Pressure Simcenter STAR-CCM+ simulates various pressure quantities, depending on the model selection. Reference Pressure The reference pressure P ref is a device that reduces the numerical roundoff error in the numerical calculations involving pressure. For more information, see Setting Reference Values . Working Pressure The working pressure is the solution variable p that is used in the flow models in Simcenter STAR-CCM+ . In the code and in the documentation, this variable is mentioned most of the time as pressure. Its definition is a matter of computational convenience and depends on the model selection. It is always expressed relative to the reference pressure P ref . In laminar flows without the influence of gravity, pressure is equal to the static gauge pressure. If the gravity model is active, pressure is equal to the piezometric pressure, as shown in Eqn. (738) for variable and constant density flows. When a turbulence model is used, working pressure includes the contribution of 2 3 ρ k . This contribution can be considered negligible in most flows of engineering interest. Static Pressure The static pressure P stat is the spherical part of the stress tensor acting in fluids. In Simcenter STAR-CCM+ , the static pressure is given by: P s t a t = P abs - P ref where P abs is the absolute pressure and P ref is the reference pressure. The static pressure is often termed the gauge pressure in literature. It also happens that the static pressure is the same as the thermodynamic pressure, which is one of the state variables. Hydrostatic Pressure The hydrostatic pressure P hydro is the pressure exerted by a fluid in equilibrium at a given altitude within the fluid due to the force of gravity: P hydro = ρ ref g ⋅ ( x − x 0 ) ⁢ where: ρ ref is the reference density. g is gravity. x is the altitude. x 0 is the reference altitude. Absolute Total Pressure The absolute total pressure P t , abs is the pressure that results from isentropically bringing the flow to rest in the absolute frame. For ideal gas: P t,abs = P abs ( 1 + γ − 1 2 M 2 ) γ ⁢ / ( γ − 1 ) where γ is the ratio of specific heats and M is the Mach number.
